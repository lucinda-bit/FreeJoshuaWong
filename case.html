Who he is

Joshua Wong has campaigned for democracy in Hong Kong since he was a schoolboy. At fifteen he stopped a Beijing-backed “national education” curriculum; at seventeen he was the face of the Umbrella Movement, on the cover of TIME as “The Face of Protest”; at twenty he was the subject of an award-winning Netflix documentary. At thirty, he is in a maximum-security prison.

What his latest sentence is for

“Conspiracy to collude with foreign forces” under the National Security Law, a law imposed by Beijing in 2020.

  • The evidence is his own words: his meetings with foreign politicians, social media posts, op-eds and interviews.
  • Much of it predates the law: prosecutors relied on his lobbying from before the law existed, and on later posts and interviews to argue it carried on.
  • He was already in prison: he was charged from his cell, while still serving another sentence.
  • The result: ten more years, handed down on 5 November 2026.

He is serving a sentence for believing us.

1984

Britain gave its word

The UK signed the Joint Declaration and guaranteed Hong Kong’s freedoms for fifty years. The Government has itself declared China in breach of it.

1996

Britain was responsible for him the day he was born. It still is.

Joshua was born under British rule, shortly before the handover. His generation was promised fifty years of freedom. Britain must keep its promise.

2026

It has reached our streets

Two men were convicted at the Old Bailey this year of spying on Hong Kong dissidents in Britain, and jailed. Hong Kongers settled here have bounties on their heads.

His life so far

From 15 to 40

Download the fact sheet

Joshua was born on 13 October 1996, so each age shown is his age at the time.

Oct 1996Born

Born in Hong Kong

Joshua Wong Chi-fung grows up in a city promised a high degree of autonomy until 2047.

Sep 2012Age 15

Defeats “national education”

With Scholarism, the student group he co-founded the year before, he leads the protests that force the Hong Kong government to shelve a compulsory “national education” curriculum.

Sep 2014Age 17

The Umbrella Movement

His arrest at Civic Square helps spark 79 days of street occupation demanding genuine universal suffrage. Time puts him on its cover.

Apr 2016Age 19

Co-founds Demosistō

With Nathan Law and other Umbrella Movement leaders, he launches Demosistō, a new political party campaigning for democracy in Hong Kong.

Sep 2016Age 19

Gets his friend Nathan Law elected

Too young to stand himself, Joshua campaigns for Demosistō’s chairman, Nathan Law, who wins a seat on Hong Kong Island and becomes the youngest lawmaker in Hong Kong’s history, at 23. Nathan is disqualified the following year.

Oct 2016Age 19

Detained at Bangkok airport

Held and deported by Thai authorities on his way to speak at a university, an early sign of how far Beijing’s pressure would reach.

Jan 2017Age 20

The film

Joshua: Teenager vs. Superpower premieres at Sundance and wins the World Cinema Documentary Audience Award.

Aug 2017Age 20

First prison sentence: unlawful assembly

Jailed for six months for unlawful assembly over the 2014 Civic Square protest. Released on bail after about two months, in October 2017; Hong Kong’s top court quashes the sentence in February 2018.

2018–19Age 21–22

Second prison term: contempt of court

Jailed for contempt of court over the 2014 clearance of the Mong Kok protest site. He serves the sentence in separate periods, the last in May–June 2019.

Sep 2019Age 22

Testifies to the US Congress

Gives evidence to the Congressional-Executive Commission on China on human rights in Hong Kong. Years later, prosecutors cite his lobbying in Washington against him.

Jun 2020Age 23

The National Security Law

Beijing imposes the National Security Law on Hong Kong. His party, Demosistō, disbands the same day.

Nov 2020Age 24

Jailed over protests, and never released

Held in custody from 23 November after pleading guilty, he is sentenced in December to 13½ months for unauthorised assembly over a 2019 protest outside police headquarters. More protest sentences follow in 2021, including for joining a Tiananmen vigil. He has not been free since.

Jan 2021Age 24

Subversion case: arrested as one of the Hong Kong 47

Arrested in prison under the National Security Law for taking part in an unofficial pro-democracy primary election, and charged with subversion in February.

Nov 2024Age 28

Subversion case: 4 years 8 months

Sentenced in the largest national security trial in Hong Kong’s history. His release date is set for early 2027.

Jun 2025Age 28

Collusion case: charged again in prison

Charged with conspiracy to collude with foreign forces, carrying a maximum of life imprisonment. The evidence is his own advocacy and public statements.

Sep 2026Age 29

Raised in Parliament, then a guilty plea

On 1 September Sir Iain Duncan Smith raises his case in the Commons and the Foreign Secretary says he will take it up. The next day Joshua pleads guilty in the collusion case. On 13 September, 31 MPs, peers and rights leaders sign an open letter to the Prime Minister.

13 Oct 2026Age 30

Turns thirty in prison

Parliamentarians mark his birthday with a screening of the new film in the Houses of Parliament.

5 Nov 2026Age 30

Collusion case: ten more years

A Hong Kong court sentences him to ten years for conspiracy to collude with foreign forces. Ten governments, including the UK, condemn it in a joint statement. Taiwan and the EU condemn it too.

10 Nov 2026Age 30

Raised again in the Commons

Five days after the sentence, Emily Darlington MP asks the Government to summon the Chinese Ambassador and call for Joshua’s release. The next day she and seven other MPs and peers set out the same demands in a letter to The Times.

c. 2036Age 40

Release, if nothing changes

Unless governments act, Joshua may not walk free until he is around 40, having spent most of his adult life in prison.
