Unjust prosecution

A letter to the editor of The Times from MPs and peers across the parties, published on 11 November 2026.

Letter to the editor · The Times · 11 November 2026

Sir,

Your leading article is right to ask what Britain will do for Joshua Wong (“Supine Silence”, Nov 6). The government has joined nine allies in calling for an end to “unjust prosecutions” under Hong Kong’s national security law, but condemnation is a statement, not a policy. Joshua Wong, who has been sentenced to ten years’ imprisonment, stood up for the Sino-British joint declaration, a treaty Britain signed and registered at the United Nations, which our own government says China has breached. A treaty broken without cost invites the next breach.

The government should summon the Chinese ambassador, as it did for Jimmy Lai; sanction those responsible for national security law prosecutions, as the US has; and tell parliament what further consequences will follow if Jimmy Lai, Joshua Wong and Benny Tai are not freed.

Published in The Times: read it on thetimes.com (subscription may be needed). The letter responds to The Times’s leading article, “Supine Silence”.

Signed by

  • Bobby Dean MP
  • Lord David Alton of Liverpool
  • Lord Patten of Barnes
