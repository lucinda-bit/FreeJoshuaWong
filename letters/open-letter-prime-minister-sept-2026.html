Britain should stand for something

An open letter to the Prime Minister, Andy Burnham, sent on 13 September 2026.

Awaiting a reply from Downing Street
Open letter to the Prime Minister · 13 September 2026

Dear Prime Minister,

This week, you said ‘it is more important than ever that Britain stands for something.’ The Foreign Secretary went further: that governments ‘have to make our own decisions about legality, values’ and act on them.

We agree. We are asking you to apply that principle to Hong Kong and the Joint Declaration, and hold China to account for its abusive use of the National Security Law, criminalising peaceful democratic activity.

Joshua Wong has been a pro-democracy advocate since he was a child. At 15 he founded a movement to resist Beijing’s education law and went on to become the face of the Umbrella Movement. His work always sought to hold Beijing to the 1997 agreement, ‘One Country, Two Systems’, agreed between the UK and China. But in 2020 he was arrested and detained. Now, like British Citizen Jimmy Lai, he faces the prospect of life in prison.

This is not a distant problem. Two men were convicted at the Old Bailey this year for spying on Hong Kong dissidents in Britain, reportedly also monitoring British parliamentarians. Police are investigating footage appearing to show Chinese embassy security in London brandishing banned electroshock weapons at peaceful protesters. Hong Kongers settled in the UK have bounties on their heads. China’s National Security Law is felt on the streets of Britain.

The UK has a moral and diplomatic responsibility, arising from the 1997 handover and the Joint Declaration, to speak out for those who stood up for the treaty it guaranteed. The UK Government has already found China in breach of the treaty. In December, the last Foreign Secretary told the Commons plainly: “China breached the Joint Declaration by imposing its National Security Law on the city.”

It is time that you called the Chinese Ambassador to raise Joshua’s case and that of the others imprisoned for standing up for democracy.

Britain signed the Joint Declaration and guaranteed Hong Kong’s freedoms for fifty years. Joshua Wong only ever asked his government to keep its word. We are asking ours to keep it too.

Written before Joshua was sentenced on 5 November 2026.
