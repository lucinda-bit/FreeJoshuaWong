The case in 30 seconds

He is serving a sentence for believing us.

At fifteen, Joshua forced the Hong Kong government to drop a school curriculum written in Beijing. At seventeen he was the face of the Umbrella Movement. Everything he campaigned for came back to one thing: holding Beijing to the promises Britain negotiated and guaranteed in 1984.

He has been in prison since November 2020. He was due out in January 2027. Instead, he was charged again under the National Security Law, and on 5 November 2026 a Hong Kong court gave him ten more years. The evidence was his own public words, including testimony to parliaments.

What Britain should do

Three asks

Britain gave its word on Hong Kong. Statements matter. Action matters more.

Raise Joshua’s case with the Chinese Ambassador

The Prime Minister should call in the Ambassador and raise Joshua and the others imprisoned for standing up for democracy, by name.

Review the Hong Kong trade office’s privileges

The Hong Kong Economic and Trade Office in London keeps diplomatic-style privileges, even after a senior manager there was convicted of spying on Hong Kongers in Britain.

Sanction those responsible

Hold to account the officials behind National Security Law prosecutions, working with Britain’s allies.
